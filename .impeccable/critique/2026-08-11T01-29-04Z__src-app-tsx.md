## Anti-Patterns Verdict

Is this AI slop? No, and not close. Hand-derived palette with measured contrast per face, a mascot, a non-library dial with angle hit-testing, copy with a voice. Zero gradients, zero glass, no hero-metric block, no icon-grid, no identical card grid. Every absolute ban clear.

Deterministic scan: 2 findings, both at `src/index.css:192`, both about the typeface.

- `overused-font`: Plus Jakarta Sans is on the saturated list.
- `design-system-font`: font not declared in DESIGN.md. False positive; DESIGN.md is explicitly superseded and says so in its own header.

The first is not a false positive, and it lands harder than the detector knows. PRODUCT.md rejects "Inter/system-font-only typography" by name. Plus Jakarta Sans is the next shelf over in the same aisle. CLAUDE.md already lists four verified peso-carrying alternatives (Quicksand, Lexend, Mulish, Work Sans) and Quicksand or Mulish would carry "playful, warm" harder than Jakarta does.

Second-order reflex check, the one that bites: "personal budget app that isn't corporate fintech" resolves to soft pastels, generous radii, a cartoon animal, flat white. That is exactly where this landed. It is an unusually well-built instance of a saturated lane, not an escape from one. First-order reflex avoided, second-order not.

PRODUCT.md also rejects "evenly-distributed rainbow palettes" by name. Five equal-weight pastels handed out by rotation (`addCategory` takes the next unused ink) is that. Colour carries no meaning beyond "not my neighbour."

## Overall Impression

Craft is far ahead of product. The token system, contrast reasoning, and copy are better than most shipped apps. But the app is a live calculator with an archive you cannot use, and the primary action writes into that dead archive.

Biggest single opportunity: the type scale is flat, and it is flattening the one number that matters.

## What's Working

The copy. "Over by 8% · ₱2,400 more than this payday." "Hit Save payday and this is where it lands." "Already your split." Every string names an object or a consequence. Nothing restates a heading. Professional-grade UX writing.

Destructive-action discipline. Three dialogs, all `alertdialog`, all with focus trap, Escape, focus return, and `aria-describedby` pointed at the real consequence text. `role="status"` mounted empty so the over-allocation banner actually announces. Rare to see done right.

The over-allocation stance. Never clamped, reported in three registers (banner, card foot, dial centre), all in `--action-edge`. A deliberate design position, held consistently.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The type scale is nine steps in an 11 to 29px range, and the payday figure loses

`tailwind.config.js` ships label 11, meta 13, ui 15, body 16, title 19, total 20, amt 23, hero 20 to 24, sec 29, wordmark 24.

Adjacent ratios: 15 to 16 is 1.07, 19 to 20 is 1.05, 23 to 24 is 1.04. Shared laws want at least 1.25; the product register wants 1.125 to 1.2. Three steps are invisible.

The consequence is not abstract. `text-hero` maxes at 24px. `text-amt` is 23px. The take-home figure, the number every other figure is derived from, is the same size as the amount on each category card, and there are three to five of those. The source number is outweighed by its own derivatives, N to 1. It also ties the wordmark exactly at 24px, so brand and money read at one level.

The config comment says the deck figures went down a rung "so the deck leads without shouting." It stopped leading.

Fix: collapse to six steps. Drop `ui` (15) into `meta` or `body`; 1px is not a step. Merge `total` into `title`. Push `hero` to a real clamp with a 34 to 40px ceiling so the payday clears `amt` by at least 1.4. Keep `sec` for the templates h1.

Suggested command: `/impeccable typeset`

### [P1] Save payday writes a record nothing can read back

`savePaycheck` stores salary plus a full breakdown. `HistoryPanel` renders it and offers exactly one action: delete. No row is clickable. No restore. The comment at `PaydayHeader.tsx:116` even says the sync effect handles "loading a saved payday from history"; that path does not exist.

Compounding: the breakdown string is `max-[700px]:hidden`, so on a phone a history row is a peso figure and a date and nothing else.

The app's one primary action produces a write-only archive, and on the device this is most likely opened, the archive holds almost no information.

Fix: make a history row restore the payday amount on click; the salary alone is most of the value and costs one callback. Then decide whether restoring the split is a feature or a trap. On mobile, replace the hidden breakdown with the top category rather than nothing.

Suggested command: `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] localStorage failure is silent, and it loses the user's split

`storage` swallows every error and returns `null`. A user with disabled or full localStorage gets defaults on every reload, edits them, closes the tab, and returns to defaults again. No message, no warning, no clue. Silent data loss in a tool whose entire state is local.

This is the app's only real failure mode and the only one with no handling at all.

Fix: have `storage.set` report failure once and surface a single persistent line, for example "Your split isn't being saved, this browser is blocking local storage." One string, one state.

Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] No undo, and deleting one item costs exactly what wiping everything costs

Deleting one sub-item, deleting a category with six items, and replacing the entire split from a template all cost the same thing: one dialog. Nothing is recoverable. History captures categories only inside a saved payday, so it is not a backup either.

Friction should be graded, and at least the top of the range should be reversible.

Fix: keep the last replaced or deleted state in a ref and offer "Undo" in the `role="status"` region already mounted for the over-allocation banner. One slot, already wired, already announced.

Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] The dial promises an interaction it does not have

A circle cut into wedges and pulled apart says "grab a wedge." It is read-only; hover lights it and that is all. The most distinctive object in the app is a chart.

Meanwhile the same three figures (name, percent, peso) appear in the legend and on every category card at different sizes. The screen prints its data twice and lets you edit it in only one of those places.

Fix: either drag-to-resize a wedge (the angle math in `wedgeAt` already exists and already reads eased slices), or accept it as a readout and shrink the legend to earn its space back.

Suggested command: `/impeccable shape`

### [P2] Arrow keys are dead in every number field

`NumberField` and the payday field are both `type="text"`, for a real reason: React's loose compare on number inputs strands a leading zero. But the cost was never paid back, since no `onKeyDown` restores stepping. Adjusting a category from 30 to 35 percent is select-and-retype, every time, on the app's single most repeated interaction.

This is most of why heuristic 7 scores 1.

Fix: `onKeyDown` on ArrowUp and ArrowDown for plus or minus 1, Shift for 10. Roughly six lines, keeps the text input.

Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

Jordan, first-timer on a phone: sees a populated 50/30/20 split at zero pesos, which is good. Then nothing tells them sub-item percentages are of the category, not the salary. They put "Rent 40" under a Needs category that is 50 percent of salary and get 20 percent of their pay, silently. The one genuinely confusable model in the app is explained nowhere. Opens history to check a past payday and gets a number and a date; breakdown is hidden at their width.

Alex, power user adjusting the split every payday: no keyboard shortcuts, cannot arrow a percent, cannot reorder categories (wedge order is creation order, permanently), cannot duplicate a category, cannot restore last month's payday to compare. Every session is retyping.

The PRODUCT.md persona, casual, opens it to feel in control of their money: gets that, right up until they hit Save and discover the button files their payday somewhere they can never properly look at. The peak moment of the app, the burst animation and the "Saved" flash, is attached to an action with no downstream payoff. Peak-end rule inverted: the flourish is the whole reward.

## Minor Observations

- Token drift between `CLAUDE.md` and `src/index.css`: `--r` is 12px (doc says 10), `--r-sm` is 8px (doc says 6), `--page` is `#f3f3f1` (doc says `#fafaf9`), `--control-h` is 48px (doc says 56). Worse, `index.css:147-151` states "every surface is 10px and every control is 6px" two lines above `--r: 12px`. The file contradicts itself.
- The new frost tile changed the mark family. The card head is now `bg-white/25` with an ink glyph; the legend pip is still a face fill with an ink glyph. The two marks no longer key to each other by fill, which was the thing tying a card to its wedge. Reads fine in isolation; worth checking with five cards on screen.
- `overflow-x: hidden` on `body` hides a layout bug rather than fixing one.
- History caps at 50 and the 51st silently evicts the oldest, with no notice.
- `IconButton` sets `aria-label` and `title` to the same string; some screen readers announce both.
- Saving a zero-peso payday is allowed and writes a useless history row.
