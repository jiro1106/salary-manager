# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A single person managing their own payday: deciding how a peso amount splits across budget categories (needs/wants/savings, or whatever they've set up) and glancing back at past paydays. Casual, personal context, not a finance professional using it at a desk for work — it's a tool they open on their own time to feel in control of their own money.

## Product Purpose

Nala turns a payday amount into a percentage-based split across user-defined categories and sub-items, visualized as a cut-apart circle, and keeps a capped history of past paydays. Success is a screen where the whole feature set is visible at once — no scrolling to discover functionality — and a split that's satisfying to look at and adjust, not just correct.

## Brand Personality

Playful, warm, direct. Explicitly **not** corporate finance software: "fun, intuitive, a site you visit for the UI experience." A cartoon cat (Nala) is the brand mark, appearing exactly once per screen. Numbers themselves stay dead-straight and tabular — the playfulness lives in material and character, not in how the money reads.

## Anti-references

Rejected by name: purple gradients, glossy 3D SaaS blobs, untextured stock photography, icon-grid feature rows, Inter/system-font-only typography, evenly-distributed rainbow palettes. If a screen could be reskinned into any other budgeting app by swapping the logo, it has failed.

## Design Principles

- Commit fully to one aesthetic per direction rather than blending safe elements.
- The whole feature set stays visible at once on the home screen — no scroll-to-discover, no hidden functionality behind navigation.
- Over-allocation (>100% assigned) is a valid state the UI reports plainly, never silently clamps or corrects.
- The form can change (cardstock → flat white was a full rewrite); the feature set doesn't.
- Money reads as data first: tabular figures, one currency-safe typeface, no decorative treatment on the numbers themselves.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: visible focus states (currently a blue outline chosen to clear every ground colour in use), adequate text contrast, and `prefers-reduced-motion` support. No additional stated requirements.
